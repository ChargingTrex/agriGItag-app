## Finding 2: photos never upload while the phone clock runs fast

`uploadPhotos` (`photo_uploader.dart`) compared the server-time `expires_at` of a presigned URL with the phone clock,
so on a phone 14 min or more fast every fresh URL looked stale and no PUT ever ran. It now reads the stored skew
(`clockSkewKey` = `clock_skew_ms`, written by `_recordSkew` on every push, which now uses the same constant) and checks
`expires_at` against `now − skew + 1 min`. Chosen over "drop the pre-check and treat a 403 as expired": it is 3 lines,
keeps the cheap pre-check, and does not spend a whole ~300 KB PUT on 2G to learn that a URL expired. The skew is only
stored above 10 min (else 0), which still leaves ≥ 4 min of a 15 min URL at the 1 min margin.

## Finding 3: a running sync keeps pushing A's entries with B's token

New `SyncContext.userChanged()` re-reads the Session row. `pushEntries` checks it before every batch; `uploadPhotos`
before the URL refresh (an authed re-push) and before every PUT round; `_confirm` before every confirm call. On a
change the phase returns and A's rows stay `waiting` under A (nothing is failed). The in-flight case the audit names
(`api_client.dart` `onError`: a 401 retried with the *current* token, which is now B's) is closed at its root: the
interceptor does not re-send when the current token's `sub` differs from the sent one's. `jwtSub` (session.dart) is
the one JWT `sub` decoder, shared by `openSession`, `_adoptStored` and the interceptor. Pull and bundles are not
guarded: what they fetch with B's token is wiped by the next run's D-029 user-switch reset.
